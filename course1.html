1. Course Description

Research Methodology Fundamentals: From Curiosity to Researchable Inquiry is an eight-week intensive introductory course designed to transform students from passive consumers of research knowledge into beginning researchers capable of thinking systematically about research problems.

Unlike conventional research-methodology courses that often emphasize terminology and memorization, this course introduces research as a way of thinking, questioning, reasoning, evaluating evidence, and producing knowledge. Participants move progressively from identifying an area of curiosity to understanding research paradigms, locating and critically reading scholarly literature, identifying research problems and gaps, developing research questions and objectives, understanding theory, selecting broad methodological approaches, considering sampling and ethics, and ultimately developing and defending an original research concept note.

2. Course Philosophy

The central philosophy: Research cannot be learned only by learning about research. Research is learned by doing research.

  • 30% — LEARN: Concepts, principles and examples
  • 40% — PRACTICE: Exercises, workshops and research labs
  • 20% — PRODUCE: Participants develop their own research ideas
  • 10% — REFLECT & RECEIVE FEEDBACK: Peer review, instructor feedback and revision

3. Overall Course Aim

To develop participants' foundational research literacy, critical inquiry and research-thinking competencies so that they can independently transform an area of interest into a coherent, theoretically informed, ethically responsible and methodologically feasible research concept.

4. Course Objectives

  • Develop an understanding of research as a systematic process of knowledge production.
  • Develop a research-oriented way of thinking based on curiosity, evidence and critical questioning.
  • Understand major philosophical assumptions and paradigms underlying research.
  • Distinguish among quantitative, qualitative and mixed methods approaches.
  • Transform broad interests into researchable problems.
  • Search for, evaluate, organize and synthesize scholarly literature.
  • Identify meaningful research gaps.
  • Formulate coherent research questions and objectives.
  • Understand the roles of concepts, variables, theory and conceptual frameworks.
  • Develop foundational understanding of research design and sampling.
  • Recognize major ethical issues and principles of research integrity.
  • Understand responsible and inappropriate uses of generative AI in research.
  • Integrate these competencies into a research concept note.
  • Communicate and defend a research idea academically.

5. Learning Outcomes

  • LO1 — Research Literacy: Explain the nature, purposes, characteristics and stages of systematic research.
  • LO2 — Research Thinking: Distinguish a general topic from a researchable problem and critically interrogate assumptions and evidence.
  • LO3 — Philosophical Understanding: Explain at an introductory level how ontology, epistemology and research paradigms influence methodological decisions.
  • LO4 — Literature Competency: Develop basic search strategies, locate credible academic literature, critically read research articles and organize literature systematically.
  • LO5 — Gap Identification: Distinguish several types of research gaps and formulate a defensible preliminary gap based on literature.
  • LO6 — Question Development: Develop logically aligned research problems, questions and objectives.
  • LO7 — Theoretical Thinking: Explain the role of concepts and theory and construct a preliminary conceptual representation of a research problem.
  • LO8 — Methodological Reasoning: Distinguish quantitative, qualitative and mixed-methods approaches and justify a broad methodological direction appropriate to a question.
  • LO9 — Ethical Research: Recognize fundamental ethical and integrity issues in research.
  • LO10 — Research Communication: Develop, present and defend a coherent research concept note.

7. WEEK 1 — BECOMING A RESEARCHER

Theme: What Is Research and Why Do We Do It?

Session 1 — Research Beyond the Textbook

What is research? | Research versus information gathering | Research versus common sense | Scientific and systematic inquiry | Characteristics of rigorous research | Basic versus applied research | Exploratory, descriptive and explanatory purposes | Academic versus policy/applied research | Research as knowledge production | What researchers actually do

Session 2 — The Research Journey

Research curiosity → problem → literature → gap → question → theory → methodology → evidence → interpretation → dissemination.

Introduces: Research process, Research cycle, Iteration, Uncertainty, Critical thinking, Researcher's mindset.

Research Lab / Activity

Example: Statement: "Social media is harmful to students."

Ask: Is this a research finding, assumption, opinion or hypothesis? What evidence would you need before accepting it?

8. WEEK 2 — HOW RESEARCHERS KNOW

Theme: Paradigms, Evidence, and Ways of Knowing

Session 3 — How Do We Know What We Know?

Everyday examples for: Ontology, Epistemology, Methodology, Methods.

Session 4 — Major Research Paradigms

Positivism, Postpositivism, Interpretivism, Constructivism, Critical approaches, Pragmatism.

Demonstration: Research Question → Philosophical Position → Methodological Approach → Methods

Research Lab / Activity

Phenomenon: "University students' experiences with generative AI."

Groups develop questions from positivist/postpositivist, constructivist, critical, and pragmatic perspectives.

9. WEEK 3 — FROM INTEREST TO RESEARCH PROBLEM

Theme: Finding Something Worth Investigating

Session 5 — Topic ≠ Research Problem

StageExample
AreaEmployment

Session 6 — Building a Problem Statement

Context | Magnitude/nature of problem | Existing knowledge | Missing knowledge | Consequences | Research justification | Feasibility | Originality | Social/scientific significance

Research Lab / Activity

Problem Tree: Causes → Core Problem → Consequences, then transformed into researchable language.

10. WEEK 4 — ENTERING THE SCHOLARLY CONVERSATION

Theme: Finding and Reading Research Literature

Session 7 — How Researchers Search

Google Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, discipline-specific databases | Keywords, Synonyms, Boolean operators, Phrase searching, Truncation, Citation chasing | Seminal vs. recent literature.

Session 8 — How Researchers Read

Extracting: Question → Theory → Method → Sample → Findings → Limitations → Contribution

Research Lab

Create a Literature Matrix (Citation | Question | Context | Theory | Method | Sample | Findings | Limitations | Relevance). Introduction to Zotero.

11. WEEK 5 — FINDING THE GAP

Theme: Moving From "What We Know" to "What We Need to Know"

Session 9 — What Is a Research Gap?

Types of Gaps: Evidence gap, Knowledge gap, Population gap, Contextual gap, Methodological gap, Theoretical gap, Contradictory evidence, Temporal gap, Practice/policy gap.

Session 10 — From Literature to Argument

Summary vs. Synthesis (Moving away from shopping-list style to thematic synthesis).

Research Lab

Develop: Known → Unknown → Why Unknown Matters → Proposed Contribution.

12. WEEK 6 — ASKING THE RIGHT QUESTION

Theme: Research Questions, Objectives, Concepts and Theory

Session 11 — From Gap to Research Question

Question Characteristics (FINER/PICOT/SPIDER frameworks) → General objective → Specific objectives.

Session 12 — Concepts, Variables and Theory

Concept, Construct, Variable, Independent/dependent variable, Operationalization, Theoretical & Conceptual frameworks.

Research Lab

Develop primary/secondary questions, general/specific objectives, key concepts, and a preliminary conceptual map.

13. WEEK 7 — HOW COULD WE STUDY IT?

Theme: Introduction to Research Design, Sampling and Ethics

Session 13 — Choosing an Approach

Quantitative, Qualitative, Mixed methods (Survey, Case study, Phenomenology, Ethnography, Grounded theory, etc.).

Session 14 — People, Sampling, Ethics & AI

Sampling strategies (Probability vs. Non-probability) | Ethics (Consent, Confidentiality, Risk) | Integrity (Plagiarism, Fabrication) | Responsible AI usage rules.

14. WEEK 8 — FROM STUDENT TO BEGINNING RESEARCHER

Theme: Integrating and Defending the Research Idea

Session 15 — Writing a Research Concept Note

Integration of Title, Background, Problem, Literature, Gap, Questions, Objectives, Framework, Methodology, Significance, and Ethics.

Session 16 — Research Idea Defence

5–7 minute presentation followed by 5 minutes Q&A mini academic conference format.

15. Final Capstone

Research Concept Note: 2,500–3,000 words. (Serves as input for Course 2 proposal development).
