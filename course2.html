1. Course Description

Applied Research Design: From Idea to Research-Ready Proposal is an intensive, hands-on course designed for students, early-career researchers and professionals who understand basic research methodology but need the practical competencies required to design and prepare an implementable research study. The course moves participants systematically from an initial research concept to a complete research proposal. Particular emphasis is placed on aligning the research problem, questions, objectives, theory, design, sampling, measurement, instruments, data-collection procedures, analysis strategy and ethical considerations.

Rather than learning research design as a collection of definitions, participants design their own study throughout the course. Quantitative and qualitative participants undertake pathway-specific activities where necessary, while mixed-methods participants learn how the two components can be meaningfully integrated. By completion, participants should possess a research-ready proposal, data-collection instrument, preliminary analysis plan and ethics documentation that can be further adapted for a thesis, funded research project, institutional ethics submission or independent study.

2. Course Philosophy

The guiding principle should be: A good research proposal is not a document—it is the blueprint of a defensible study.

Students therefore progress through:

CONCEPT → DESIGN → OPERATIONALIZE → SAMPLE → MEASURE/EXPLORE → INSTRUMENT → PILOT → ANALYZE → PROTECT → PROPOSE → DEFEND

  • 20% Conceptual instruction
  • 50% Research-design laboratory
  • 20% Independent project development
  • 10% Peer/faculty feedback

This course should feel more like a research design studio than a conventional classroom.

3. Overall Course Aim

To develop participants' applied research-design competencies so that they can independently transform a research concept into a coherent, feasible, ethically responsible and methodologically defensible research proposal ready for implementation.

4. Course Objectives

The course aims to enable participants to:

  • Refine research problems, questions and objectives for methodological alignment.
  • Select research designs appropriate to different questions.
  • Translate abstract concepts into measurable or investigable constructs.
  • Formulate and appropriately use research hypotheses.
  • Develop conceptual and theoretical frameworks.
  • Define target populations and sampling strategies.
  • Apply basic quantitative sample-size reasoning.
  • Apply qualitative sampling and sample-adequacy principles.
  • Design high-quality questionnaires, interview guides and FGD guides.
  • Understand measurement, validity, reliability and qualitative rigor.
  • Design practical data-collection procedures.
  • Develop quantitative and/or qualitative analysis plans.
  • Prepare research ethics and data-management documentation.
  • Pilot, evaluate and revise research instruments.
  • Prepare realistic research timelines and budgets.
  • Integrate all study components into a complete proposal.
  • Critically review research proposals.
  • Orally defend methodological decisions.

5. Learning Outcomes

On successful completion, participants will be able to:

  • LO1 — Alignment: Demonstrate logical alignment among problem, gap, questions, objectives, theory and methods.
  • LO2 — Design: Select and justify an appropriate quantitative, qualitative or mixed-methods research design.
  • LO3 — Conceptualization: Develop appropriate theoretical/conceptual frameworks and operationalize key concepts where relevant.
  • LO4 — Sampling: Define a study population and construct a defensible sampling and recruitment strategy.
  • LO5 — Instrumentation: Develop research instruments aligned with research questions and data needs.
  • LO6 — Quality: Apply principles of reliability, validity, trustworthiness and methodological rigor.
  • LO7 — Data Collection: Develop a feasible fieldwork/data-collection protocol.
  • LO8 — Analysis: Develop an analysis plan before collecting data.
  • LO9 — Ethics: Identify ethical risks and prepare appropriate consent and data-protection procedures.
  • LO10 — Proposal Development: Produce and defend a complete research-ready proposal.

7. WEEK 1 — From Research Concept to Design Blueprint

Theme: Is Your Research Idea Ready to Become a Study?

Session 1 — Diagnosing the Research Idea

Review: Research problem, Research gap, Research questions, Objectives, Significance, Scope (Do not reteach Course 1).

To be introduced: Research Alignment Chain (Problem → Gap → Question → Objective → Data Needed → Design → Analysis → Conclusion)

Participants learn to detect misalignment. Example: Question: How do nurses experience workplace violence? but Method: Closed-ended prevalence survey. Ask: Can that design actually answer, "how do they experience"?

Session 2 — Building the Design Blueprint

Introduce a one-page Research Design Canvas containing: Problem, Gap, Question, Objectives, Concepts, Population, Data required, Potential design, Sampling, Data source, Instrument, Analysis, Ethics.

Lab

Participants diagnose their own research concepts.

Assignment-1

8. WEEK 2 — Choosing the Right Research Design

Theme: Let the Question Choose the Design

Session 3 — Quantitative Designs

Introduce applied use of: Cross-sectional studies, Longitudinal studies, Cohort concepts, Case-control concepts, Experimental designs, Quasi-experimental designs, Correlational studies, Evaluation research.

Emphasize: When to use → strengths → limitations → what conclusions are justified. Teach particularly: Association ≠ causation.

Session 4 — Qualitative & Mixed-Methods Designs

Qualitative: Phenomenology, Case study, Ethnography, Grounded theory, Narrative inquiry, Qualitative description.

Mixed methods: Convergent, Explanatory sequential, Exploratory sequential. Teach students not to label every interview study "phenomenology."

Research Lab

Groups receive research questions and must: Choose design, Defend choice, Identify limitations, Suggest alternative.

Assignment-2

9. WEEK 3 — Concepts, Variables, Hypotheses & Frameworks

Theme: What Exactly Are You Investigating?

Session 5 — Concepts to Measurement

Teach: Concept, Construct, Variable, Indicator, Dimension, Operational definition.

For quantitative participants: Independent variable, Dependent variable, Confounder, Mediator, Moderator. Levels of measurement: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, Ratio.

Session 6 — Hypotheses & Framework Development

Hypotheses: Null, Alternative, Directional, Non-directional. When hypotheses are appropriate and when not to force hypotheses into qualitative research.

Then: Theoretical framework, Conceptual framework, Logic model where appropriate.

Lab

  • Quantitative track: Construct a variable/measurement map.
  • Qualitative track: Construct a concept/sensitizing-concept map.
  • Mixed-methods track: Develop an integrated conceptual model.

Assignment-3

10. WEEK 4 — Population, Sampling & Sample Adequacy

Theme: Who Can Provide the Evidence You Need?

Session 7 — Quantitative Sampling

Target population, Accessible population, Sampling frame, Unit of analysis, Inclusion/exclusion criteria.

Probability sampling: Simple random, Systematic, Stratified, Cluster, Multistage. Non-probability: Convenience, Quota, Purposive, Snowball.

Then introduce sample-size reasoning: Confidence level, Margin of error, Expected proportion, Power concepts, Non-response allowance.

Session 8 — Qualitative Sampling

Purposive sampling, Criterion sampling, Maximum variation, Homogeneous sampling, Typical case, Critical case, Snowball, Theoretical sampling.

Discuss: Saturation, Information power, Sample adequacy, Recruitment strategy.

Lab

Participants build a Sampling Decision Tree.

Assignment-4

11. WEEK 5 — Instrument Development

Theme: Turning Questions Into Data

Session 9 — Quantitative Instrument Development

Questionnaire structure: Demographic questions, Knowledge questions, Attitudes, Behaviours, Likert scales, Existing validated measures, Self-developed measures, Question order, Skip patterns.

Common mistakes: Leading questions, Double-barrelled questions, Ambiguity, Double negatives, Loaded wording, Overlapping categories, Unbalanced response options.

Session 10 — Qualitative Instrument Development

Interview guides, FGD guides, Observation guides. Teach: Opening questions, Transition questions, Key questions, Probes, Follow-up questions, Sensitive questions, Closing questions.

Critical distinction: Research question ≠ interview question.

Research Lab

Students develop their actual instruments.

Assignment-5

12. WEEK 6 — Quality, Reliability, Validity & Piloting

Theme: How Do You Know Your Instrument Works?

Session 11 — Quantitative Quality

Validity: Face, Content, Construct, Criterion. Reliability: Internal consistency, Test-retest, Inter-rater. Cronbach's alpha (what it can and cannot tell you). Measurement error, Bias, Social desirability, Recall bias.

Session 12 — Qualitative Rigor

Credibility, Dependability, Confirmability, Transferability, Reflexivity. Strategies: Triangulation, Member reflection/checking, Audit trail, Peer debriefing, Thick description, Reflexive journal.

Piloting and Pretesting: Why pilot? Who participates? Cognitive interviewing, Instrument revision, Field feasibility.

Activity

Participants conduct a micro-pilot (Quantitative: 3–5 questionnaires; Qualitative: 1 mock interview).

Assignment-6

13. WEEK 7 — Designing Real-World Fieldwork

Theme: A Methodology Must Work Outside the Proposal

Session 13 — Fieldwork Planning

Recruitment, Gatekeepers, Access, Scheduling, Research team, Training data collectors, Field supervision, Quality control, Participant retention, Researcher safety, Field documentation.

For online research: Online surveys, Online interviews, Digital consent, Privacy considerations.

Session 14 — Data Management

File naming, Participant IDs, De-identification, Data storage, Backups, Password protection, Access control, Version control.

Quantitative: Codebook, Data entry, Range checks. Qualitative: Audio files, Transcription, Translation, Anonymization, Transcript management.

Lab

Participants construct a Fieldwork Workflow: Recruitment → Consent → Data collection → Quality check → Storage → Backup.

Assignment-7

14. WEEK 8 — Planning Analysis Before Collecting Data

Theme: Know How You Will Analyze Before You Collect

Session 15 — Quantitative Analysis Planning

Question → Variable → Data type → Analysis. Introduce: Descriptive statistics, Frequency, Mean/median, Cross-tabulation, Chi-square, t-test, ANOVA, Correlation, Regression concepts.

Session 16 — Qualitative Analysis Planning

Introduce: Data familiarization, Coding, Categories, Themes, Interpretation. Briefly distinguish: Thematic analysis, Content analysis, Framework analysis, Narrative approaches, Discourse analysis, Bibliometric analysis.

Lab

Create a Question-to-Analysis Matrix (Research question | Data required | Source | Instrument | Variable/concept | Proposed analysis).

Assignment-8

15. WEEK 9 — Ethics, Feasibility, Budget & Timeline

Theme: Can and Should This Study Actually Be Done?

Session 17 — Applied Research Ethics

Discuss cases involving: Children, Gender-based violence, Sexuality, Health, Trauma, Illegal activities, Workplaces, Online communities, Power relationships, Incentives.

Prepare: Participant information sheet, Consent form, Assent, Recruitment script, Confidentiality procedure, IRB concepts, Protocol deviations, Data protection.

Session 18 — Feasibility & Project Planning

Teach: Research timeline, Gantt chart, Budget, Personnel, Travel, Transcription, Software, Participant costs, Contingency, Dissemination.

Assignment-9

16. WEEK 10 — Proposal Development & Defence

Theme: Turning Design Into a Defensible Research Proposal

Session 19 — Writing the Proposal

Structure: Title, Background, Problem Statement, Literature Review, Research Gap, Research Questions, Objectives/Hypotheses, Theoretical/Conceptual Framework, Methodology, Ethical Considerations, Limitations, Timeline, Budget, Dissemination, References, Appendices.

Session 20 — Research Proposal Defence

Formal defence: Each participant gets an 8-minute presentation followed by a 7-minute defence before a small panel of researchers.

Assignment-10

17. Final Capstone

Complete Research-Ready Proposal: Suggested length of 4,000–6,000 words (excluding references and appendices).

Must include: Research instrument, Sampling plan, Analysis plan, Ethics documents, Timeline, Budget.

20. Final Proposal Rubric

  • Problem-gap alignment — 15%
  • Research questions/objectives — 10%
  • Theoretical/conceptual coherence — 10%
  • Design appropriateness — 15%
  • Sampling — 10%
  • Instrument/data collection — 10%
  • Analysis plan — 10%
  • Ethics — 10%
  • Feasibility — 5%
  • Academic writing/referencing — 5%

21. Proposal Defence Rubric

Assess: Research rationale, Methodological reasoning, Internal alignment, Feasibility, Ability to respond to criticism.

22. The Research Design Portfolio

Each participant leaves with 19 structured items including: Research Design Canvas, Frameworks, Sampling Plan, Tested Instrument, Pilot Report, Data Management Plan, Ethics Package, Timeline, Budget, Complete Proposal & Presentation.

23. Two-Track Labs

Beginning around Week 3, students are divided into Quantitative Lab and Qualitative Lab while retaining common conceptual sessions. Mixed-methods participants can attend both or receive a dedicated integration clinic.

24. Peer Review System

Structured peer reviews evaluating problem clarity, design alignment, instrument capability, sampling justification, analysis plan, and ethical risks.

25. Research Clinics

5 optional 60–90 minute clinics across the 10 weeks: Problem Alignment, Framework/Design, Sampling, Instrument, Final Proposal.

26. Templates Provided

Includes complete Applied Research Design Toolkit with templates for Design Canvas, Question-Objective Alignment Matrix, Operationalization Table, Sampling Planner, Instrument Guides, Ethics Forms, Gantt/Budget forms, and Defence Rubrics.

27. Suggested Software Exposure

Zotero/Mendeley (References), Google Forms/KoBoToolbox/REDCap (Data collection), SPSS/R/Stata (Quantitative concepts), NVivo/ATLAS.ti/MAXQDA (Qualitative concepts).

28. Research Integrity

Signed Research Integrity & Responsible AI Declaration confirming data non-fabrication, verified sources, verified AI usage, and participant privacy protection.

29. Certificate

  • Certificate in Applied Research Design: ≥80% attendance, all major assessments completed, ≥60% overall, instrument & proposal completed and defended.
  • With Distinction: ≥80% overall score.
